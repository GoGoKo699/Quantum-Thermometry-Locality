# Related work and scope

## The problem addressed here

The temperature parameter is encoded only in the initial Gibbs state of one semi-infinite XX spin chain. An arbitrary thermometer can interact with the first $`r`$ bath sites, while the remaining chain bonds retain their fixed strength $`J`$. The apparatus, controls, ancillas and detector are temperature independent; feedback can depend on the observed record. The bath is not re-prepared.

For a positive one-particle band and inverse temperature in a fixed compact interval, the [main result](../science/model_and_results.md) is

```math
\mathcal R_b(r,\tau)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{1}{r+\tau}\right),
\qquad b=\beta J,\quad \tau=Jt,\quad r\geq1,
```

with $`\hbar=k_{\mathrm B}=1`$. The lower bound includes arbitrary probe number, entanglement, retained memory, adaptive measurement and control strength within the accessible block. The upper bound uses immediate occupation readout when $`r`$ dominates, and a fixed counted sensor at one endpoint when elapsed time dominates.

The relevant distinction is therefore the **complete controlled experiment with a microscopic, spatially restricted thermal source**. An inverse-time precision law, finite propagation speed, and collective enhancement within a Markov model are established ideas. They are not separate novelty claims of this work.

## Mathematical and physical contribution

The result combines two statements that must hold in the same model. First, every allowed experiment receives only the distinguishability contained in an initial region of size proportional to $`r+\tau`$, up to a controlled truncation error. Second, a fixed local sensor can continuously convert enough of that distinguishability into an observed count without replacing the thermal bath. The matching bounds identify the optimal global scaling.

The uniformity in the thermometer's size and control strength is physically consequential: increasing those resources at a fixed contact does not increase the asymptotic supply of initially encoded temperature information. Enlarging the contacted thermal region does. The theorem therefore separates accessible bath volume and elapsed propagation time from the number of auxiliary probes.

The limitations are substantive. The matching construction uses an integrable one-dimensional spin chain with a strictly positive band and a fixed compact positive inverse-temperature interval. The work establishes neither a pointwise QFI bound nor a uniform low-temperature result. It does not exclude improvements of constants, address arbitrary bosonic baths, or determine a sharp finite-coupling crossover. The [physical consequences](../science/physical_consequences.md) give the operational collective-Markov implication with those qualifications.
